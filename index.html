Research question

Does the sales-to-new-listings ratio improve six-month forecasts of median resale prices across different GTA municipalities?

What do these forecasts imply for monthly mortgage-payment affordability for prospective buyers under different mortgage-rate and household-income scenarios?

2Motivation

Why our project matters

A home is the largest purchase most households make, and its monthly cost can change a lot in half a year.

  • Prices change a lot in six monthsIn our test period, a municipality's median price moved about 9% on average over six months, up or down.
  • Monthly payments have doubledThe monthly payment on a median-priced GTA home has more than doubled since 2013. Most of the rise came from higher prices and the rest from a higher mortgage rate.
  • Price predictions need to improveBuyers, lenders and policymakers all have to guess where prices will go next. A better forecast means fewer costly surprises for them.

4Benchmark and method

How we tested it

  1. Step 1Two models and AR(1) Price-only model: recent six-month price growth and the time of year.Listings model: the same, plus the sales-to-new-listings ratio.AR(1) benchmark: last month's price growth, forecast one month at a time for six months.
  2. Step 2Forecast using past data onlyEach month from January 2020 to December 2025, we fit the price-only model, the listings model and AR(1) on the data available at that time. Each one then forecasts price growth over the next six months.
  3. Step 3Compare with what happenedEach model makes 1,080 forecasts, one for each of 15 municipalities in each of 72 starting months. RMSE gives more weight to big misses and MAE is the average miss. Lower error is better.

8Conclusion

What we found

The evidence supports our claim, with limits. Adding the ratio made forecasts more accurate than both benchmarks, most clearly from 2023 on.

  • Forecasts were closer on averageThe average miss (MAE) fell from 9.20 to 8.61 percentage points and was lower in 12 of 15 municipalities. RMSE was the lowest of the three models, at 11.27.
  • The direction was right more oftenThe listings model correctly called whether prices would rise or fall 66% of the time, up from 61% for the price-only model.
  • Clearly better in the post-COVID yearsIn 2023 to 2025, RMSE fell from 8.47 → 6.97, about 18% lower. In 2020 to 2022 the ratio did not help (13.72 → 14.33).
  • Better payment estimates as wellWith the same mortgage rates, the listings model called the direction of the monthly payment correctly 71% of the time, up from 67%.
Limits
  • The median price also moves when the mix of homes sold changes.
  • The test covers one period, 2020 to 2025, which includes the pandemic.
  • The overall gain is small and its 95% interval includes zero, so it may not hold in other periods.
  • Only the 15 largest municipalities and a six-month horizon were tested.
  • Mortgage rates and income are assumptions that the viewer chooses.
  • One national mortgage rate is used for every municipality.
  • The model does not capture how a change in rates would itself move prices.
  • Payments leave out property tax, insurance and fees.
